# Live Workout

- The exercise three-dot menu uses a grouped Forge sheet titled Exercise options, without repeating the exercise name. Its title uses the standard sheet-header role and its section headings use the shared section-title role. Log & setup presents Tracking, Add/Edit/View note and Warm-up sets as three side-by-side tiles; Explore groups Previous sessions and Exercise info; Change exercise groups Replace exercise and Mark off-session/Count in session. Existing permissions and availability determine which actions appear, and empty groups are omitted. Remove exercise remains the destructive header action. Labels may wrap with larger text, and the content scrolls when it exceeds the sheet height.

- The Live Workout timer strip uses the light-grey card surface in Light and its elevated dark surface in Dark, so Elapsed and Rest remain grouped visibly against the page.

- Set-number badges have the same compact 34 pt square size for every set, with the set number or kind letter centered. Press tint matches only the painted badge bounds and corners, while its touch target remains at least 44 pt. Properties use separate corner dots in the same theme-aware ink colour, inset toward the center: next-session target at top-left, RIR at bottom-left, and set comment at bottom-right. RIR presence includes zero; its numeric value remains available in set details and the accessible indicator label. No annotation row appears below the measurements. Exercise-level notes remain beside the exercise name, separate from set comments.

- The bottom navigation dock may compact after a meaningful downward scroll and expands only after a meaningful upward scroll; reaching the top alone never expands it. It remains visible and restores its full size when touched or when navigating to another tab. Primary tab changes use a short cross-fade plus one persistent selection indicator moving to the active item; they never use a push or lateral slide. Retapping the active tab preserves its current navigation and scroll position. Its state changes should be immediate and smooth, without an additional compact-to-expanded entrance transition. During that height change, the active selection pill stays centred within its measured tab slot and contracts with it; it never retains an expanded capsule or shifts sideways. It remains visually separated from the bottom system indicator with a light lower fade. Starting a workout smoothly expands the dock to its five-item width; it contracts again when the workout ends. An active workout replaces the dock's generic play glyph with the workout type's semantic icon, without applying the type colour to dock chrome. The internal dock never duplicates the rest countdown; iOS presents it in the system-owned Live Activity.
- The Live Workout list must reserve enough bottom space for the persistent dock so the dashed-outline `Add exercise` action remains reachable after adding many exercises.
- Dashboard's Start workout action first opens an animated Forge source chooser. `Blank workout` creates an active workout and lands directly on the Workout tab with the animated Add exercises picker already entering. `From routine or plan` replaces that chooser with the same in-place routine/plan selection flow used for historical workouts; selecting a routine starts its independent active-workout snapshot without redirecting to Plans. The blank path never flashes an empty workout tab before the mandatory picker.
- Elapsed and rest timers derive from timestamps and refresh together from one second-aligned wall clock; a restart publishes its exact start instant before rendering, so its first visible value is the selected duration rather than one second high. Active-workout persistence is debounced during ordinary edits, while finishing or cancelling remains immediate. A rest timer is scoped to its active workout, so stale persistence cannot overwrite a new set-completion restart.
- Tapping Elapsed opens a compact Workout duration sheet with Forge-owned, platform-neutral start-date and start-time pickers, an Automatic Time Tracking switch, and Reset Start Time. Picker choices remain drafts until Done; Back, Close, and dismissal leave the start timestamp unchanged. Automatic tracking is on by default for new and legacy workouts; turning it off freezes the current elapsed value, and turning it back on resumes from that value. Reset Start Time is a destructive confirmed action that resets the live duration to zero.
- Tapping Finish saves empty workouts immediately, preserving their originating routine. Completed routine workouts with no routine decision also finish immediately. When an unchanged routine has incomplete sets as its only finish decision, use Forge's centered confirmation card with Keep working out and Finish with N incomplete sets; include unilateral sets with only one completed side. A structurally changed routine-derived session uses the larger Finish workout sheet and requires an explicit choice to keep the original, update it, or save as a new routine. Deleted source routines offer save-workout-only or save-as-new. Nonempty workouts started without a routine always offer History only or Save as routine, even when all sets are complete; the latter saves the session to History and creates a reusable standalone routine. Routine-saving choices use the larger sheet with name, duration, exercise count and completed/total sets. Its fixed header places Close on the left and the same check-only filled Finish control as the Live Workout header on the right, retaining disabled validation, loading feedback, duplicate-submit protection and inline error handling. The check-only action has a full accessible name: Finish with N incomplete sets for incomplete sessions, otherwise Finish workout. Finishing preserves partially completed sides. Close, Back and dismissal keep the workout active; one final save has no subsequent success popup. Successful finishing returns directly to Dashboard without flashing a no-active-workout screen. Existing destructive discard confirmation remains separate.
- The active Rest timer keeps its countdown and adjustments in a compact shared sheet: a large timer, linear progress rail, two equal quick actions, then a destructive Stop timer control. Before expiry the actions are `−10s` and `+10s`; once overdue they become recovery actions (`+10s`, `+30s`) and the elapsed value turns destructive red. Extending an expired rest restarts it from its programmed duration plus the adjustment, so a completed 10-second rest plus 10 seconds becomes a 20-second countdown rather than carrying its overdue time forward. A timestamp-derived owner persists the active end time locally, drives the sheet, schedules one local end-of-rest notification when Rest timer is enabled, uses the user’s sound preference for that notification, and gives foreground completion the configured success haptic. Native builds use Forge's bundled `alarm.wav` for the end-of-rest alert, matching the previous Forge app; Expo Go cannot bundle that custom notification sound and uses its normal development fallback. On an iOS development or production build, Forge's local Swift ActivityKit extension renders that date interval on the Lock Screen and Dynamic Island with Forge's bundled rest-timer glyph and Manrope Semibold, transitions from neutral to Forge amber in the final fifth and red when overdue, and opens the Rest timer through `forge://rest-timer/open`; the Dynamic Island uses the matching subtle keyline tint because iOS owns its black background. It is not available in Expo Go or an unre-built binary, where Forge silently uses the normal timer and notification fallback. Android continues to use its native notification/channel fallback, including the Forge timer glyph and destructive-red completion accent. iOS owns standard local-notification chrome and app icon presentation. When `Keep rest timer running` is enabled, the timer continues after zero as an overdue `+m:ss` value until stopped or adjusted.
- Sets and exercise cards reveal compact contextual delete actions with a left swipe; the action remains hidden in the resting state. When open, the destructive surface appears behind the card's retained trailing curve.
- The Add exercises picker dismisses its keyboard when its list is touched or dragged, without preventing the requested list action or dismissing the picker. A downward pull on the shared top drag tab, the X, or the modal backdrop closes it; list content never participates in dismissal dragging.
- Exercises added to a live workout or routine start with three empty sets. Live exercise tables retain their complete Set, Previous, measurement, and completion grid when prior values are absent; empty prior values render as a neutral dash rather than causing the measurement columns to move.
- Live Workout exercise cards retain the saved canonical exercise name and, when the immutable snapshot has it, show concise `Variation · Primary equipment` metadata below it. Metadata never repeats a variation already expressed by the name; legacy snapshots remain name-only. In an expanded Add Exercises family, Forge presents controlled multi-select modifier rows in compact semantic Forge cards (Load, Grip, Width/stance, Assistance method, and so on), plus named standalone variants rather than a generated preset list. A load mode plus one movement detail resolves automatically into one canonical or stable derived exercise; selections from unrelated families stay separate. Two independent movement details (such as Wide and Neutral) or conflicting values open an output chooser instead of being silently joined. Weighted and Assisted are mutually exclusive load modes in a single result, while Assisted may combine with a compatible grip, width, setup, laterality, and explicit Machine/Band method. Source-specific variation prose remains a standalone exercise rather than becoming a guessed modifier.
- Canonical exercise metadata removes generic `Standard`/standalone `Machine` details and any equipment or variation already expressed by the title. A recognised legacy exercise is upgraded to the v3 canonical title and snapshot without changing its entered sets, notes, completion, or position; unknown/custom exercises remain intact.
- The bundled Strength catalogue stays intentionally compact: Forge's reviewed baseline is the default, while the Kinetic source snapshot contributes only explicitly curated missing movements. Source-specific prescriptions, novelty drills, aliases, and near-duplicates do not enter the picker just because they occur upstream. Supported load, grip, stance, setup, and laterality choices remain available through the family facet resolver, so breadth does not require a long preset list. The reproducible curation and audit commands reject malformed, duplicate, noisy, or unclassified records before a snapshot is shipped.
- The Live Workout pencil opens the full-screen workout editor, reusing the Forge History editor surface rather than the former short sheet. It works on a deep local draft containing name, type, notes, attributes, exercises and sets, but deliberately omits Date, Start time and End time: active-workout timestamps and duration remain owned by Live Workout. Its exercise cards use the same collapsed Routine Editor presentation and interactions, including the long-press Forge drag handle, no completion state, and no per-exercise options; movement updates only the draft, gives the held card a restrained floating treatment and auto-scrolls as needed. Existing next-session targets remain advisory data from recorded training rather than routine configuration. Save commits the complete draft atomically to the active workout, while Cancel, the system Back gesture and route removal prompt `Discard changes?` only when the draft changed. Set changes made there never start, stop or adjust Rest timer; logging on the Live Workout screen remains its sole timer owner.
- If a focused weight or reps field would sit too close to the number keyboard, Live Workout and the Routine Editor temporarily scroll it into the comfortable middle of the visible area. They use native keyboard insets where available and their prior scroll position returns when the keyboard is dismissed.
- Exercise options use Forge action-list and decision patterns. Tracking opens a compact grouped internal selection view with Back, without redundant field-requirement instructions; an explicit per-block tracking override takes precedence over snapshot tracking and legacy/default tracking without changing catalogue defaults. Their note editor replaces the action list in-place, with Back and an X rather than a separate Done action; notes grow with their content. A saved exercise note appears only as a chat indicator directly after its canonical name, without changing card height or exposing note text. Previous sessions show the newest three matching completed workouts as detailed Forge cards, expand with Show N more, and open the selected read-only workout detail. The sheet closes before that native-stack push, then on Back reappears in place — restoring its current internal view, expanded list, and scroll position without replaying its pop-up entrance, so returning reads as the same full surface rather than a fresh sheet. Options also provide catalogue information and—when the movement has an absolute-weight field—an editable warm-up ramp that inserts tagged Warm Up sets before working sets. The ramp pre-fills from the latest completed working set when one exists, while remaining editable. Removing an exercise from this menu requires a Forge destructive confirmation; swipe-to-delete remains the fast contextual delete affordance.
- Set-number chips open a Forge bottom sheet with a centred set number, an optional single set kind, a grouped Session note & effort card, and a closely paired grouped Next session target card. These actions preserve all other edits. The comment input keeps an 80 pt height and scrolls internally for longer text, without a character limit. The sheet fits the available section content and scrolls when necessary; typing a longer comment never expands the field or the sheet. Applicable Next session target fields sit side by side with their labels above the inputs, wrapping within the available width. Each field focuses its matching numeric/punctuation keypad input; it derives its visible fields and labels from the exercise tracking type (for example, Assisted kg + Reps or Reps alone) and never alters the recorded set. A single next-session target is decimal and locale-aware: a comma is a decimal point, not a range separator, and the separator the user typed is preserved on blur (`82,5` stays `82,5`, `82.5` stays `82.5`) rather than being rewritten to a period. When the next active workout starts from the same routine, the latest completed workout's targets replace only their matching exercise and set's planned fields, including weight and/or a reps, duration, or distance range. They do not mutate the reusable routine and are naturally one-use: the following start reads the most recently completed session instead. History uses the same grouping as static content without change controls. RIR, comments and next-session targets remain integrated as distinct accessible corner dots inside the uniformly sized badge. Exercise notes similarly use an end-of-name indicator without changing card height. Exercise note editing starts at 156 pt and grows for longer text. - Tracking is configurable per exercise in a routine or workout. Loaded strength offers Weight + reps or Weight + time; bodyweight offers Reps or Time; weighted/assisted bodyweight preserves its load field; Cardio offers Distance, Time or Distance + time; loaded carries offer Weight + distance or Weight + time. Numeric keypads use their decimal separator as the clock separator, and pasted colons remain supported. Time displays mm:ss below an hour and hh:mm:ss above it, and persists seconds; routine duration ranges remain seconds-based. Recorded reps support fractional repetitions, including halves and quarters, with a decimal keypad. A comma or period is a decimal separator (`8,25` and `8.25` both record 8.25 reps); entered notation is preserved on blur and reload. Measurement and target fields retain append/backspace entry with the cursor locked at the right edge.
