## Findings

### 1. HIGH: Header overflows at 320px and an equivalent enlarged layout

Status: OPEN. Rule: R-03; C-4. Reason: the navigation exceeds the available page width, clipping the right side of the theme control.

Locations: `website/styles.css:65`, `website/styles.css:69`, `website/styles.css:94`, `website/styles.css:345`.

Reproduction: load the landing page using each configured phone browser profile, then set the viewport to 320px. The fixed horizontal header gaps, brand and navigation do not reflow. WebKit measured document widths of 323px in Light and 326px in Dark against a 320px viewport; the navigation right edge measured 323.45px and 326.42px. Chromium expanded its layout viewport to 323px/326px despite the requested 320px width, which can mask the overflow if a test compares only `scrollWidth` with `innerWidth`.

At a 640px viewport with CSS `zoom: 2`, WebKit measured document widths of 647px/653px and navigation right edges of 646.89px/652.83px. This is a reproducible enlarged-layout probe, not evidence of native Safari text scaling or an actual browser-menu zoom test.

Recommendation: reflow the header at the content's breaking width, while keeping every action and its touch area available. Do not hide overflow or shrink hit areas to mask the problem. Account for both theme-label lengths. Recheck all three pages because they share this header.

Evidence: `/tmp/forge-website-audit/results.json`, `iphone-light-320.png`, `iphone-dark-320.png`, `galaxy-light-320.png`, `galaxy-dark-320.png`, and the corresponding `*-200-percent.png` captures. The four 320px captures were inspected.

### 2. HIGH: Several navigation hit areas are smaller than the skill's 44px target

Status: OPEN. Rule: R-03; human/layoutmobile companions. Reason: the text links and logo are harder to activate reliably by touch.

Locations: `website/styles.css:76`, `website/styles.css:99`, `website/styles.css:291`, `website/styles.css:352`.

The brand link is 34px tall in both phone profiles and approximately 37.4px tall on desktop. Privacy is 39.5px wide on iPhone Air and 40px wide on Galaxy S24, despite its 44px height. The footer GPL link is 43.3px/42px wide. These are standalone navigation controls, not inline prose links. They occur on all three pages.

Recommendation: give these standalone controls at least 44px in each dimension without enlarging the visible logo/type unnecessarily. Resolve alongside finding 1 so the added hit-area padding does not worsen header overflow. This is the antislop/Forge target-size bar, not a claim that every sub-44px target automatically fails WCAG 2.2 AA; spacing and inline exceptions affect that standard.

Evidence: `results.json`, each page's measured `controls` array, plus the inspected normal page and 320px captures.

### 3. HIGH: A failed theme script leaves an enabled control that does nothing

Status: OPEN. Rules: R-26, R-27, R-34; C-4. Reason: a visible button promises a theme change but has no fallback or unavailable state when its script is absent.

Locations: `website/index.html:34`, `website/index.html:52`, `website/index.html:251`, `website/site.js:12`, `website/theme.js:1`. Privacy and Support repeat the same script/button arrangement.

Reproduction: block only the `site.js` request while leaving `theme.js` and CSS available. In both phone profiles and both themes, clicking the enabled theme button left `data-theme` unchanged. In Light, screenshot URLs also remained the hard-coded Dark versions. In Dark, the static label still said Dark theme rather than describing the opposite action. With JavaScript disabled entirely, both system preferences produced the Light CSS palette and Dark screenshots.

The normal, successfully loaded state works: Space and Enter operate the toggle, screenshots switch, preference survives reload, and all pages use the selected theme. This finding concerns a deliberately reproduced failed-script state, not a claim that normal theme switching is broken.

Recommendation: make the control available only once its behavior is attached, or provide an explicit unavailable state. Supply a coherent static/system-theme screenshot fallback. Keep article content and links usable when scripts fail; a static marketing page does not need invented loading spinners or application-style empty states.

Evidence: `/tmp/forge-website-audit/edge-cases.json`, `*-blocked-script.png`, and the `noJS` observations in `results.json`. The Light iPhone failure capture was inspected and confirms the Light page/Dark screenshot mismatch. Dark failure screenshots can capture images mid-load; the recorded source URL and before/after theme values establish this finding rather than a blank-image appearance.

### 4. LOW: Explore does not identify the navigation destination

Status: OPEN. Rule: R-15. Reason: the generic label hides that this link opens the app screenshot gallery.

Locations: `website/index.html:34`, `website/privacy.html:34`, `website/support.html:34`.

The desktop Explore link correctly navigates to `/#screens`; the destination is a six-screen preview gallery. Suggested label: Screenshots. The link is hidden in the mobile header, so this is a desktop/tablet copy improvement rather than a mobile dead-link finding.

Evidence: `/tmp/forge-website-audit/external-links.json`, final entry. This is a clarity correction, not a reason to rewrite the accepted first-person author voice or redesign the site.

## Verification and click-through

Checks ran at `nice -n 10`, four concurrent phone/theme contexts, within the repository's two-thirds CPU allowance. One browser audit batch ran at a time. No TypeScript source was changed, so a new app typecheck or unrelated app suite was not needed.

- iPhone Air/WebKit and Samsung Galaxy S24/Chromium: landing, privacy and support in both themes; 12 full-page captures with fonts and images loaded.
- 72 recorded internal-link clicks across those contexts. Home reaches the site root; Support and Privacy reach their pages; Get Forge reaches `#install`; Read the privacy policy reaches `privacy.html`; support's installation link reaches `#install`.
- Desktop Explore reaches `#screens`. External iOS builds, Android builds, iLoader, Source code, GPL licence, GitHub privacy statement and Forge issue tracker were each clicked and returned HTTP 200 at the expected destination. No issues, comments, forms, builds or messages were submitted. Repeated labels sharing one external destination were represented by one click per unique URL.
- Skip link receives first Tab focus. Enter changes the fragment to `#main`; the following Tab reaches Get Forge in all four phone/theme contexts. A `BODY` active element immediately after the fragment change was not falsely reported as broken focus progression.
- Theme button focus has a visible 3px solid outline. Space switches to the opposite theme; Enter switches back; reload retains the choice.
- Reduced-motion emulation reports `0s` button transition duration.
- No website `pageerror` events in normal states. Intentional script blocking is separately recorded.
- Before this audit, Lighthouse after image optimization scored performance 96, accessibility 100, best practices 100 and SEO 100, with 2.4s simulated LCP. That run preceded the final hero-panel removal. It is historical supporting evidence, not a new audit run or a guarantee of field performance. Findings 1 and 2 illustrate why the automated accessibility score is not exhaustive.

Raw observations, probes and captures are in `/tmp/forge-website-audit/`. That directory is local evidence and may be removed by system cleanup. The measured results above are retained in this report.

## Purpose and identity record

- Colour: Forge's Light/Dark neutral family connects the public pages to the app; the primary CTA uses tonal contrast as the deliberate accent.
- Typography: self-hosted Manrope reuses the app's recognizable type family and avoids external font requests.
- Hero: journal copy and an actual workout preview introduce the product rather than a generic illustration. The user's removed background panel stays removed.
- Feature layout: four unboxed peer topics describe recording, preparation, review and import; cards are not added merely for decoration.
- Gallery: equal image columns support comparison of six real screens; mobile stacks them instead of compressing text into narrow columns.
- Spacing: broad product sections separate the gallery, data handling and installation; document pages use a constrained reading width.
- Shape: pill controls signal actions, modestly rounded images preserve app presentation, and sections remain unboxed.
- Motion: only brief control feedback, with reduced-motion support; no decorative perpetual animation.
- Assets: existing Forge logo and explicitly requested screenshot captures; fictional sample training is disclosed, with no fabricated testimonials, customers or performance claims.

## Limits and recommendations that do not override Forge

Browser profiles do not establish native app behaviour, native screen-reader behaviour, true mobile text scaling, installation/signing success or app-store policy compliance. CSS zoom is an approximation; real browser text-only scaling remains unverified. The existing legal copy was checked for correspondence with documented app behaviour, not reviewed as legal advice. No new dependencies are needed for the confirmed recommendations.

Antislop is advisory and does not authorize replacing Forge's identity, reintroducing the rejected hero backdrop, generating new branding, adding an application framework, or changing app navigation. The 44px hit-area recommendation should enlarge invisible interaction space without unnecessarily changing visible type/logo size. Static marketing content does not require invented database-empty or remote-fetch-loading UI. Theme-script fallback is a real resilience issue and is separately reproduced.

Knowledge: no durable knowledge change. This report and its audit-index link are advisory evidence only. No Forge rules or accepted product requirements changed during the audit.
