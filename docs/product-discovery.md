# Product discovery

## Product purpose

Forge is a quiet, fast strength-training tool that helps people record workouts, sets, weights, reps, rest periods, and progress without distracting them from training.

The product should leave the user feeling in control, productive, calm, and clear. It is a dependable personal training instrument, not an entertainment or coaching product.

## Primary user

People who regularly strength-train or weightlift and want a simple, private, focused workout tracker rather than a social fitness platform or coaching service.

## Problem

Many fitness apps add accounts, subscriptions, social features, coaching, analytics, and navigation that slow down the simple act of logging a set. Forge should make recording extremely fast between sets, especially one-handed and while tired, while keeping data local and under the user's control.

Core product constraints:

- No accounts required.
- No ads.
- No tracking.
- Local-first, private workout data.
- Strength training is the clear center; cardio, court sports, and duration-based activities are secondary modes.

## Main user journey

1. Open the app.
2. Resume the active workout if one exists; otherwise browse the Dashboard and choose a routine, plan routine, or blank workout.
3. Log sets directly in a stable continuous workout workspace.
4. Optionally use a user-controlled rest timer.
5. Finish with a lightweight summary, without forced review or coaching.
6. Find the completed workout in chronological History and use it as future context.

## Product personality

The experience should feel premium and precise while remaining understated and platform-neutral. It should be refined and intentional without looking like a stock iOS app or a Material Design app.

It should not be mistaken for a bodybuilding coaching app, social fitness network, gamified habit tracker, spreadsheet, or generic system utility.

## Product non-goals

- Motivational feeds or social interaction.
- Coaching that decides weights, reps, or rest for the user.
- Badges, scores, streak pressure, or celebratory gamification.
- Analytics that compete with the actual workout record.
- Rigid routines that make deviation feel like failure.

## Dashboard personality

The Dashboard should feel like a polished training archive: a calm, refined record of the user's training history with a prominent useful calendar. It is not a “today's planned workout” screen and should not assume future scheduling.
